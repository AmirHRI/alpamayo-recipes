# Layer mixing instead of expert pruning

**Result: keeping the teacher's full 36-layer action expert and synthesising its cache from
the 2B student's 28 layers beats pruning the expert to 28, by 27.1% min_ade (z −13.69) for
+9.1% latency at 1 NFE. Learning the mixing matrices does not help; a fixed depth-proportional
map is what pays.**

Companion to `PRUNING.md`, which measured what pruning costs. This measures the alternative.

## The problem

HF indexes `past_key_values` by `layer_idx`, so **expert layer *l* reads VLM cache layer *l***
and the expert's depth is pinned to the VLM's. `AlpamayoR1.__init__` derives the expert config
from `vlm.config.text_config`, so a 28-layer Cosmos-Reason2-2B builds a 28-layer expert, and
the teacher's 36-layer expert arrives through a load-time remap that drops 8 layers (set C,
`{4,10,13,15,19,25,27,34}`).

`PRUNING.md` prices that cut before the student is even involved:

| teacher through | min_ade |
|---|---|
| its own full 36-layer expert | **0.5776** |
| the set-C 28-layer ablation | **0.7893** |

So every 2B block/span arm has been optimising toward a ceiling that pruning lowered by 0.2117.

## The alternative

Keep all 36 expert layers. Synthesise their cache slots from the student's 28 with
block-convex matrices — `models/layer_mix.py`:

```
student VLM layers   0..6      7..13     14..20    21..27      (4 blocks of 7)
                       |  P_K/P_V |         |         |         (each 7x9)
expert cache slots   0..8      9..17     18..26    27..35      (4 blocks of 9)
```

`P = softmax(logit, dim=g_in)`, so every output slot is a convex combination of the 7 student
layers in its block. 2 x 4 x 7 x 9 = **504 scalars**. Block locality is structural, which is
what makes the span loss at `m = g_out` grade one block at a time: `_span_sweep`'s disjoint
spans over 36 layers are exactly `[0, 9, 18, 27]`.

Supervision is the existing pair, unchanged: teacher-forced block loss (m=1) plus the span
loss at m=9, weighted mean.

### Three properties that made this cheap

**`_block_loss` needed no changes.** It is written in terms of `len(expert.layers)` and indexes
`student_kv[i]`, so a 36-entry synthesised dict drops straight in and the π-subsetting branch
simply stops triggering. The entire training-side edit is two lines after `recompute_kv`.

**A layer mix commutes with RoPE.** `cos`/`sin` are computed once per forward and shared by
every layer, and rotation is linear in K, so `RoPE(Σ aₗ kₗ) = Σ aₗ RoPE(kₗ)`. The same P
therefore applies to pre-RoPE K in training and to the post-RoPE `DynamicCache` at eval, and
the two paths cannot drift. V is never rotated. Asserted in `tests/test_layer_mix.py`, not
assumed. (Contrast the per-layer D×D basis maps `scripts/kv_linear_probe.py` warns about —
those mix the head dimension the rotation acts on, and do *not* commute.)

**P cannot hide inside the loss.** `KVProjectorBank` carries the warning at
`kv_distill.py:294-304`: a learned map living only inside `L_KV` can absorb the whole
cross-model mismatch while the tensor the expert reads never moves. P is not that — it *is*
the cache the expert consumes, in training and at eval alike.

## What the arms establish

**Keeping the expert whole is the whole win.** `mix 10x` beats pruning at every epoch on both
metrics, by −0.19 to −0.70 min_ade (z −5.7 to −17.1). Best-vs-best it is **1.0662 vs 1.4621,
−27.1%, z −13.69**, winning on 73.4% of 1000 clips. Against the 0.5776 ceiling that closes
**46%** of the gap pruning left open. The mix arms are also *monotone* on both metrics, where
the pruned baseline regressed twice (e2 and e4) — and `mix 10x` was still improving at e5, so
it was stopped by the schedule, not by convergence.

**Learning P does not help — three arms agree.** `mix 100x` moves P 12x further (a single
weight travels 23 percentage points; K and V diverge to 0.166) and is worse at *every one of
five epochs*, +0.011 to +0.039 min_ade. The direction P learns is toward more blending, which
is exactly what the teacher-mode oracle measured as harmful (one-hot `gather` beat blended
`tent` by 0.066 min_ade). The block loss cannot tell the two maps apart — identical to four
decimals at epoch 1 — so P is free to wander where the trajectory metric penalises it.

**Pinning the ends is neutral.** Wiring student 0..3 → slots 0..3 and 26..27 → slots 34..35
straight through (deepstack/ViT-injection at the head; the deep layers the causal ladder put at
~95% of the recoverable gap at the tail) and learning only the middle 22 → 30 converges to the
same place as the unpinned learned arm: 1.0923 vs 1.0904 min_ade, 2.5319 vs 2.5355 ade -- ahead
on one metric, behind on the other, both by less than a thousandth. It starts further back
(its 11 → 15 blocks are a wider span for the tent to cover, so its init approximates worse) and
catches up by epoch 2. Well-motivated, no effect.

## Downstream: EoS and CD, the deployable chain

Everything above uses the teacher's FROZEN expert -- a diagnostic. The deployable chain adds
two stages, and the layer-mix advantage survives both:

    KD block+span  ->  EoS expert-on-student (2 ep)  ->  CD endpoint gt03 (2 ep)

| stage | pruned min_ade / ade | mix min_ade / ade | paired delta (z) |
|---|---|---|---|
| frozen teacher expert, NFE=10 | 1.5030 / 3.2645 | **1.0662** / 2.4965 | -- |
| + EoS, NFE=10 | 1.3974 / 2.4859 | **1.1223** / **2.1045** | -0.2751 (-10.31) / -0.3814 (-10.18) |
| + CD, NFE=2 | 1.3344 / 2.5141 | **1.1336** / **2.1613** | -0.2008 (-8.24) / -0.3528 (-9.97) |
| + CD, NFE=1 | 1.6629 / 2.6968 | **1.3513** / **2.1013** | -0.3116 (-9.82) / -0.5955 (-13.52) |

**CD at 2 steps is nearly free**: 1.1336 vs the EoS 10-step 1.1223 (+0.011 min_ade, +0.057 ade)
-- a 5x sampler reduction for ~1%.

### ⚠️ EoS and CD both trade DIVERSITY for a better single mode

`ade` is sample 0; `min_ade` is oracle best-of-6. The spread `ade - min_ade` tracks the trade:

| | min_ade | ade | spread |
|---|---|---|---|
| mix, frozen expert (NFE=10) | 1.0662 | 2.4965 | 1.4303 |
| mix + EoS (NFE=10) | 1.1223 | 2.1045 | 0.9822 |
| mix + CD (NFE=2) | 1.1336 | 2.1613 | 1.0278 |
| mix + CD (NFE=1) | 1.3513 | **2.1013** | **0.7500** |

EoS collapses the spread on its FIRST epoch (1.4303 -> 0.9880) and then barely moves
(0.9822 / 0.9793 / 0.9800 at e2/e3/e5) -- so epoch 2 is the best checkpoint on both metrics
and later ones are flat, independently reproducing "a third epoch buys nothing" for this
student too. At NFE=1 the spread bottoms out at 0.7500: the single draw is the best `ade` in
the whole table while `min_ade` degrades, because best-of-6 has nothing left to select from.

Which metric you care about therefore decides which stage to ship: `ade` for a system that
drives one trajectory, `min_ade` for anything that ranks several.

The EoS gain also SHRINKS as its input improves, and crosses zero on `min_ade`:

| student | control min_ade | after EoS | gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| nav2bmix original e5 | 2.6216 | 2.0753 | -0.546 |
| nav2bmix clean_maskfix | 1.5030 | 1.3974 | -0.106 |
| mix2bnav e5 | 1.0662 | 1.1223 | **+0.056** |

That is not EoS behaving differently on the mix student -- it collapses diversity in BOTH
chains, and by MORE in the pruned one (1.7615 -> 1.0885 vs 1.4303 -> 0.9822). The net sign
depends on how much room the mode had: the pruned control's mode was bad enough that fixing it
outweighed the diversity loss; the mix control's mode is already good, so the loss dominates.

## Latency

H100, 2 cameras (1577 tokens), B=1, bf16. Prefill from `LATENCY_PROFILE.md`; the step numbers
re-measured, with the 28-layer one reproducing its 14.4 ms within 1.3%.

| stage | pruned 28-layer | layer-mix 36-layer |
|---|---|---|
| VLM prefill (2B) | 50.0 ms | 50.0 ms |
| P (28 → 36 mix) | — | 2.9 ms |
| 1 denoise step | 14.6 ms | 17.6 ms |
| **1-NFE total** | **64.6 ms** | **70.5 ms** (+9.1%) |
| 10-NFE total | 195.9 ms | 229.3 ms (+17%) |

The expert step is **depth-bound**, not cache-bound: 28 → 36 costs 1.21x, near the 1.29x layer
ratio. P runs **once on the prefill cache, never per denoise step** — so it amortises the wrong
way for 1-NFE sampling, where its 2.9 ms is half the added cost.

⚠️ That 2.9 ms is ~2/3 waste: `mix_cache` moves ~1.7 GB for a 414 MB job (stack → 4 einsums →
cat → per-slot copies), running at ~18% of HBM bandwidth. A single block-diagonal GEMM over a
`[28, B*H*T*D]` view would put it under 1 ms and cut the 1-NFE penalty to ~+6%.

## Silent failures this arm can produce

Each of these runs clean and reports finite numbers.

**A short cache does not raise against a deeper expert.** `DynamicCache.update` AUTO-EXTENDS —
verified against a real 36-layer Qwen3-VL decoder. Slots 28..35 are created on demand holding
only the 64 action tokens with no VLM prefix, and those are the layers `layer_importance.py`
measured at ~105% of the recoverable gap. Every guard below exists because of this.

**`load_alpamayo1_vlm` keeps only `vlm.*` keys.** A trained `layer_mixer.*` is dropped without
a word, so an eval would score the untrained init while reporting a trained result.
`_load_layer_mix` raises when a checkpoint has none; `_refuse_orphaned_layer_mix` covers the
reverse (a mix checkpoint evaluated with `layer_mix=False`).

**The EoS training forward does not mix on its own.** `TrainableStitchedAlpamayoR1` borrows
`TrainableAlpamayoR1.forward`, which hands the VLM cache straight to the expert and never
touches `sample_trajectories_prefill_only` where the eval path mixes. `from_student` installs a
depth-guarded forward pre-hook rather than copying the borrowed forward.

**`slurm_eval_stitched.sh`'s `*2b*` branch matches `mix2bnav_*`** and would force the pruned
config *and* export `PRUNE_EXPERT_LAYERS`. `mix2b*` / `mixpin2b*` branches override it and
unset the pin. `slurm_train_eos.sh` requires the pin; `MIX=1` unsets it.

**Pruning and mixing are alternatives, never companions.** `layer_mix` with
`PRUNE_EXPERT_LAYERS` set raises at init, as does `layer_mix` with `kv_weight > 0` (`L_KV` maps
the raw 28 layers through `build_layer_map`, a contradictory convention).

## Reproducing

```bash
# train  (PLR overrides the mixer LR multiplier; omit for the shipped 10x)
ARM=mix2bnav GPUS=2 BS=4 ACCUM=4 WARMUP=333 sbatch --gpus=2 --cpus-per-task=32 slurm_train_kd.sh

# eval one checkpoint  (CKPT is REQUIRED while the run is still training)
ARM=mix2bnav_m9w1.0 CKPT=checkpoint-7815 CAMERAS='[1,3]' \
  NAV=/data/datasets/physical_ai_av/lcdrive_physicalai_av_manifests/nav_lcdrive_val_mysubset_1k.json \
  sbatch --gpus=1 --cpus-per-task=16 slurm_eval_stitched.sh

# regenerate the tables above
python -m alpamayo1_5_distill.scripts.mix_results_table
```

⚠️ `num_train_epochs` stays at **5** even when only some epochs are wanted: the scheduler is
`cosine_warmup_with_min_lr`, so that field sets the cosine PERIOD. Lowering it changes the LR
trajectory and the comparison stops being matched. Run the 5-epoch schedule and stop early.

## Open

- **`sharpen=1.0`** (pure one-hot `gather`) is untested and is the direction every piece of
  evidence points: `gather` beat `tent` in the oracle, and both learned arms lost by drifting
  toward blending. It costs nothing at inference.
- **A longer schedule.** `mix 10x` had its largest single-epoch gain at e5.
- **Freeze P.** With learning shown not to help, the 504 parameters can become a fixed buffer:
  no `lr_multiplier` entry, no `NO_DECAY_PARAMS` case, no `layer_mixer.*` in checkpoints, and
  none of the load-time guard machinery at eval.
